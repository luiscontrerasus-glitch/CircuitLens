# 60-second pitch

A breadboard can look almost right while one misplaced wire keeps an LED dark. For a beginner, the hardest part is understanding why.

CircuitLens is an electronics debugging workbench. You review a photo, confirm the components and terminals, and compare the build with an intended circuit. The engine turns those connections into electrical nets and checks polarity, current limiting, open paths, and direct rail shorts.

Every finding includes evidence, an electrical explanation, and a corrective step. Edit the terminals and run the checks again. Seven generated experiments make the learning loop available without hardware or cloud credentials.

This MVP is honest about its boundary: photo interpretation is manual, assisted by a simple visual mask. There is no claim of automatic recognition or hardware measurement. Its foundation is a working, testable circuit engine that future evaluated vision systems can feed.

CircuitLens helps students move from guessing at wires to reasoning about circuits.
